> This is how I do the Thanksgiving turkey. Aromatics in the cavity, butter under and over the skin, and a pan of buttered vegetable broth underneath that I baste with the whole time it cooks. The bird stays moist and the pan is already most of the way to gravy.

{: .box-note}
**Serves:** about 1 lb of turkey per person, with leftovers<br>
**Oven:** 325F<br>
**Total:** roughly 15 minutes per lb, plus 30 minutes to rest

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount |
| :------ |:--- |
| Whole Turkey, fully thawed | 12 to 16 lb |
| Onion, quartered | 1 |
| Celery Ribs, cut in chunks | 2 |
| Carrots, cut in chunks | 2 |
| Butter, softened | 1 Cup (2 sticks) |
| Salt | 1 tbsp |
| Black Pepper | 2 tsp |
| Garlic Powder | 2 tsp |
| Poultry Seasoning | 2 tsp |
| Paprika | 1 tsp |
| Vegetable Broth | 4 Cups, plus more as needed |
| Butter, for the pan | 4 tbsp |

The seasoning is a starting point. Use whatever mix you like on poultry.

## Before You Start

1. Thaw the turkey in the fridge. Give it about 24 hours for every 4 to 5 lb, so a 15 lb bird needs 3 to 4 days.

2. Pull out the neck and the bag of giblets from both ends. Save the neck for the pan or for stock.

3. Don't rinse the bird. Pat it dry all over with paper towels, inside and out. Dry skin is what lets the butter stick and the skin brown.

4. Take it out of the fridge about 30 minutes before it goes in the oven, and heat the oven to 325F.

## Instructions

1. Stuff the cavity loosely with the onion, celery, and carrots. They're there for flavor and steam, not to be eaten.

2. Cut half the butter (1 stick) into tabs. Work your fingers under the skin over the breast and the tops of the thighs to loosen it, without tearing it, and slide the tabs of butter underneath.

3. Mix the other stick of softened butter with the salt, pepper, garlic powder, poultry seasoning, and paprika. Rub it all over the outside of the skin.

4. Tuck the wing tips behind the back and tie the legs together with kitchen twine so the bird cooks evenly.

5. Set the turkey breast side up on a rack in a roasting pan.

6. Heat the 4 cups of broth with the 4 tbsp of butter until the butter melts, then pour it into the pan under the turkey.

7. Tent loosely with foil and put it in the oven.

8. Baste every 30 to 40 minutes with the broth from the pan. Work fast and close the oven door between rounds, since every time it's open the oven loses heat. If the pan starts to look dry, add more broth.

9. For the last 45 to 60 minutes, take the foil off so the skin can brown. If parts get dark before it's done, cover just those spots with a small piece of foil.

10. Start checking with a meat thermometer about 30 minutes before the time in the chart below. It's done when the thickest part of the thigh and the thickest part of the breast both read 165F. Keep the probe off the bone.

11. Move the turkey to a cutting board, tent it with foil, and let it rest 20 to 30 minutes before carving. Pull the vegetables out of the cavity and toss them.

## Cook Time by Weight

These are the USDA times for a fully thawed, unstuffed turkey at 325F, which covers this one since the vegetables don't count as stuffing. If you do stuff the bird with stuffing, add roughly 15 to 30 minutes, and the center of the stuffing also needs to hit 165F.

| Turkey Weight | Unstuffed | Stuffed |
| :------ |:--- |:--- |
| 8 to 12 lb | 2 3/4 to 3 hours | 3 to 3 1/2 hours |
| 12 to 14 lb | 3 to 3 3/4 hours | 3 1/2 to 4 hours |
| 14 to 18 lb | 3 3/4 to 4 1/4 hours | 4 to 4 1/4 hours |
| 18 to 20 lb | 4 1/4 to 4 1/2 hours | 4 1/4 to 4 3/4 hours |
| 20 to 24 lb | 4 1/2 to 5 hours | 4 3/4 to 5 1/4 hours |

The chart is a guide. Opening the oven to baste adds a little time, so trust the thermometer over the clock.

## Gravy

Don't throw out what's in the pan. Strain the broth and drippings into a saucepan, skim off some of the fat, and thicken it with a slurry of flour or cornstarch and cold water. It's already seasoned from the butter and the bird.

Enjoy!
